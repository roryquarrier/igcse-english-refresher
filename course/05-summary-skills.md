# 05 — Summary Skills (R5)

*Week 5. Summary is the most **teachable** question on the paper: there is a method, and the method wins marks.*

---

## Learning goals
- Select **only** the relevant points for the stated focus.
- **Paraphrase** rather than copy.
- Write in **continuous prose** within the **word limit**.
- Use **linking words** to create flow.

---

## The method (follow it every time)

1. **Read the focus line twice.** E.g. *"Summarise the **benefits** of the bridge and the **problems** it caused."* — two lists, not one.
2. **Re-read the text with a pencil.** Every time you meet a relevant point, number it in the margin. Ignore examples, jokes, and repetition.
3. **List your points as bullets** — one line each, in your own words.
4. **Group and order** them (benefits together, problems together).
5. **Write continuous prose** using connectives: *also, in addition, furthermore, however, on the other hand, as a result*.
6. **Count words.** Stay at or under the limit. Never exceed it.

**Traps:**
- **Copying whole phrases** — quotes do not count for R5. Paraphrase.
- **Adding your own opinion** — a summary has no opinion. Ever.
- **Writing one long unbroken block** — group by theme.

---

## Worked example

Focus: *"Summarise the problems caused by the new bridge."* (limit ~70 words)

**Text extract:** *Within months, the bridge drew thousands of visitors. Residents complained that the village had become a car park. The narrow lanes, once quiet, filled with idling coaches. Bakers who had thrived on local trade found tourists bought nothing and left litter. Some families moved away; others fought the council for a barrier that never came.*

**Point list:** traffic congestion → streets used as parking → tour buses idle in lanes → local shops lost regular trade → litter → families leaving → no help from council.

**Model summary (63 words):**
> *The bridge brought serious problems. Traffic congestion increased, with village streets acting as car parks and coaches idling in the once-quiet lanes. Local businesses, especially bakers, lost regular customers because tourists spent little and dropped litter. Consequently some families left, while others were left disappointed when the council failed to provide any barrier.*

Notice: **no opinion**, **linked**, **under the limit**, and every point **paraphrased**.

## Practice (Week 5)

**Text: "The Escalator"**
> The Central–Mid-Levels escalator was built to solve a simple problem: commuting up a steep hill. For residents it became a lifeline — no more taxis, no more sweat. For shop owners below the first stop, it was ruin: foot traffic flowed overhead and never touched their doorsteps. For the city, it became a curiosity that tourists queued to ride, photographing laundry lines and neon from above. And for the young couple who opened a noodle shop halfway up, whose rent the escalator had made suddenly affordable, it was the luckiest accident of their lives.

**Task:** Summarise the **different effects** the escalator had on **three** groups of people. **(8 marks, limit 80 words.)**

## Mini-task
Take any news article this week. Write a **50-word summary** with the focus: *"Summarise the main argument, ignoring examples."* Then count your words — no marks for going over.

## Marking guide
`Selection /5` — only relevant points, none missed; `Paraphrase /5` — own words, linked, within limit.
