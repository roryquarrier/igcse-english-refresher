## Diagnostic test — Section C (Text A)

**C1 (2):** on an old tram climbing the hill / inside it, forehead against the warm glass.
**C2 (3):** tourists are curious and attentive — they "raise their phones"; commuters are indifferent — they "don't look up"; the contrast shows the journey is ordinary for locals but a spectacle for visitors.
**C3 (3):** "beaten metal" suggests a hard, flat, gleaming surface; it implies the light on the water is metallic and bright; it makes the harbour sound almost still and cold rather than welcoming.
**C4 (4):** he climbs to buy a custard tart; he eats half and saves the rest for his grandmother; she can no longer manage the hill; so he makes the trip for her, not for the view.
**C5 (3):** it gives the tram a human, unhurried quality; the climb feels slow and timeless; it reflects the boy's calm, patient relationship with the journey.
**C6 (5):** e.g. "he has lived here all his life" — shows long familiarity; "he has never once grown tired of this climb" — shows it is a repeated, habitual journey; *or* "At the top, he will buy…" implying a routine. Must explain **how** the detail proves frequency.

## Module 04 answers (Cheung Chau)

**1 (2):** a whistle blows and climbers race up the tower of buns.
**2 (3):** students, chefs, delivery riders; unpaid, not famous; locals of the island.
**3 (3):** they are ordinary people with ordinary jobs; the contrast with their "hero" status for three minutes makes them relatable; the writer admires them.
**4 (3):** in 1978 the tower collapsed; forty people were hurt; the race was banned for years.
**5 (4):** "even better" does not mean safer *instead* of exciting; the risk is now shared and understood rather than hidden; the community values control and honesty over reckless danger.
**6 (5):** e.g. "an island that knows every one of their names" — shows the community is small and close; "the village decided" — the whole community chooses together; "the risk is now shared" — collective responsibility.

## Module 08 answers (R4)

**1 (3):** "tower of buns" makes the buns sound stacked, almost architectural; it turns food into a structure to be climbed; the reader pictures something precarious and strange.
**2 (3):** "throw themselves" suggests recklessness and total commitment; it shows they do it for excitement rather than reward; it makes the climb feel desperate or fearless.
**3 (4):** the three-part list slows the sentence and feels deliberate and calm; each word answers the disaster ("small" against the collapse, "careful" against recklessness); "wrapped in safety nets" makes the danger contained; the effect is that the race feels mature rather than reckless.
**4 (5):** the tone is wry and conversational ("when you think about it"); the aside feels like the writer nudging the reader to agree; it quietly states the theme — generosity, not danger, is the point; the short final clause ends the piece on a calm, confident note.
