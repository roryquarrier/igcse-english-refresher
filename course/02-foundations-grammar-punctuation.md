# 02 — Foundations: Grammar & Punctuation

*Weeks 2–3. Grammar is not a separate exam — it is the W5 marks on every writing question, plus the difference between a clear reading answer and a muddled one.*

---

## Learning goals
By the end he can, without prompting:
- keep **tense consistent** inside a paragraph;
- match **subject and verb** (including tricky collective nouns);
- punctuate **direct speech** correctly;
- use **commas** for lists, clauses and clarity;
- choose the right **sentence type** for effect (simple / compound / complex).

---

## Lesson 1 — Tense consistency

**Key idea:** a paragraph usually lives in *one* tense. Switch only when the *time* changes — and switch deliberately.

- **Past simple** — finished actions: *He scored.*
- **Past continuous** — background action: *He was running when he scored.*
- **Present perfect** — past action with a link to now: *He has scored three goals this season.*
- **Future** — *He will score / He is going to score.*

**Worked example (fix the tense):**
- ✗ *He walks into the room and saw the trophy on the table.*
- ✓ *He walked into the room and saw the trophy on the table.* (both past)

**Practice 1** — underline any verb that breaks the tense of its sentence, then correct it.
1. She opens the door, looks around, and screamed.
2. Yesterday we are going to the beach.
3. He has finished his homework an hour ago.
4. The MTR was crowded, so we decide to walk.

*Answers: 1. screamed→screams (or opens→opened…); 2. are going→went; 3. has finished→finished; 4. decide→decided.*

---

## Lesson 2 — Subject–verb agreement

**Key idea:** the verb agrees with the **subject**, not with the nearest noun.
- *The box of old photos **is** in the loft.* (subject = box)
- *Each of the players **has** a locker.* (each = singular)
- *Neither of the boys **was** late.* (neither = singular)
- Collective nouns (*team, family, class*) take **singular** in British usage: *The team **is** ready.*

**Practice 2** — choose the correct form.
1. The list of names (was/were) long.
2. Neither the player nor the coach (was/were) happy.
3. There (is/are) three reasons for this.
4. The news (is/are) good.

*Answers: 1. was 2. was 3. are (inverted — subject is "three reasons") 4. is.*

---

## Lesson 3 — Punctuating direct speech

**Rules:**
- Speech goes in **" "** (double) or **' '** (single) — be consistent.
- The **comma or question mark sits inside** the closing speech mark: *"Where are you going?" she asked.*
- The **speaker's name stays lowercase** if the reporting clause continues the same sentence: *"Wait," he said, "I'm coming."*
- **New speaker = new line.**

**Worked example:**
```
"Give me one more minute," pleaded Sam.
"Not a second longer," replied the coach.
```

**Practice 3** — punctuate (8 marks):
```
where have you been asked his sister
at the arcade he answered quietly
you were supposed to be home an hour ago she said
i know he said but i lost track of time
```
*Model answer in `10-answer-key.md`.*

## Lesson 4 — Sentence types for effect

| Type | Shape | Use it to… |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | one clause | slow the pace, hit hard: *He stopped.* |
| **Compound** | two clauses + and/but/or/so | balance ideas |
| **Complex** | main + subordinate (because, although, when, if) | show cause, contrast, time |
| **Minor/verbless** | no verb | drama: *Silence. Then footsteps.* |

**Rule of thumb:** a long, comma-spliced sentence dulls the reader. A short sentence after a long one **pops**. Vary deliberately.

**Practice 4** — rewrite this dull passage using three *different* sentence types to build tension:
> He walked down the dark corridor. He heard a sound. He turned around. There was nothing there.
*See `10-answer-key.md` for one strong model.*

## Lesson 5 — Commas, apostrophes, semicolons

- **Comma** — lists: *I bought apples, oranges, and pears.* After an opening clause: *After the match, we ate.* Marking extra info: *My cousin, who lives in Macau, is visiting.*
- **Apostrophe** — possession (*the player's boots; the players' boots*) and contraction (*it's = it is; its = belonging to it*).
- **Semicolon** — joins two closely related clauses: *It was late; we went home.*
- **Top error in HK scripts:** *its / it's* and *your / you're*. Drill these.

**Practice 5** — insert apostrophes where needed; fix *its/it's*.
1. The boy's and girls teams both won. *(careful — where does the apostrophe go?)*
2. Its raining, and the trams in the city are its slowest when wet.
3. The dogs bones were buried in the neighbours garden.
*Answers: 1. boys' and girls' (apostrophe after each plural noun); 2. It's raining … the city's trams are their slowest when wet (fix: Its→It's, its→the city's/their); 3. dogs' bones … the neighbour's garden.*

## Week 2–3 mini-tasks
1. **Edit a passage:** take one paragraph of your own writing and mark every error using a coloured pen, then rewrite it cleanly. *(2× per week)*
2. **Error log:** start a page titled "My Top 5 Errors." Add one example each time you make a mistake. Re-read before every piece of writing.

## Marking guide
`Grammar /5` — all verbs agree and stay in tense; `Punctuation /5` — speech + commas + apostrophes correct.
