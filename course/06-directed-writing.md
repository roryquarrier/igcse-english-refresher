# 06 — Directed Writing

*Week 6. W2 (structure) + W4 (register/purpose). Covers both IGCSE routes, then a note on which to use.*

---

## Learning goals
- Match **register** to **audience** (friend vs headteacher vs newspaper reader).
- Use a **clear structure** for each text type.
- Cover **every bullet/instruction** — nothing missed.
- Open and close **appropriately** for the form.

---

## Part 1 — Register: who is reading this?

| Audience | Register | Open with |
|---|---|---|
| A friend | informal, contractions, humour | *Hi Jay — guess what happened.* |
| A teacher / headteacher | formal, polite, no contractions | *Dear Mrs Chan,* |
| A newspaper | lively, opinionated, some rhetorical questions | *Hook in the first line* |
| Unknown official | *Dear Sir/Madam* → *Yours faithfully* | formal throughout |
| Named person | *Dear Mr Lee* → *Yours sincerely* | formal but personal |

**Rule:** never mix. *"Dear Sir/Madam, how's it going?"* fails W4 instantly.

---

## Part 2 — Structures for each form

### Informal letter / email (ESL & 0500)
```
Hi [Name],
Opening — friendly line + purpose.
Para 1 — first bullet/point, developed with a detail or example.
Para 2 — second point, developed (add an opinion: I think / it'd be great if…).
Para 3 — third point.
Closing — warm sign-off: Take care / Write back soon,
[Your name]
```
**Develop every point** — one thin sentence per bullet loses marks. Add **detail, example, or opinion**.

### Formal letter (complaint / request)
```
Dear [Name or Sir/Madam],
Para 1 — purpose clearly: "I am writing to complain about…"
Para 2 — the problem + evidence.
Para 3 — the effect it had on me/others.
Para 4 — what I want done (a specific request).
Yours sincerely / faithfully, [Full name]
```

### Article (magazine / school paper)
```
[CATCHY HEADLINE]
Hook — a question, a startling fact, or a mini-anecdote.
Body — 2–3 paragraphs, each with a clear point + example.
End — a punchy final line or call to action.
```
Use **first/second person** to involve the reader: *"Ever noticed how…? You probably have."*

### Speech / talk (0500 route often asks for this)
```
Address the audience: "Good morning, everyone."
State your position early.
Three clear points, each with evidence or a short story.
Acknowledge the other side briefly — then answer it.
End with a strong, memorable close (repeat your key idea).
```

### Journal / diary entry
First person, **reflective**, past tense, emotional honesty. Not a report — an *inner* voice. *"I keep thinking about what he said…"*

### Report (ESL Paper 2 Section B)
```
[TITLE]
Introduction — what this report is about.
Findings — what you discovered (sub-headings ok).
Recommendations — what should happen next.
```
Factual, formal, no jokes.

## Part 3 — The 3-minute plan

Before writing **any** directed piece, jot:
1. **Form + audience** (register).
2. **The three or four points** you must cover (or the bullet cues).
3. **One strong opening line.**

Then write. Then **check every bullet** is covered — a missing bullet is an automatic cap on the mark.

## Practice (choose the route that matches his exam)

**0500 route:** *"Write a letter to the editor of your local newspaper arguing that Hong Kong should build more public sports facilities for teenagers. Use ideas from the article and your own experience."* **(40 marks across two tasks.)**

**ESL route:** *Your school is organising a charity fun run. Write an email to a friend inviting them, covering these three bullet points: (1) what the event is, (2) why you think it will be fun, (3) what they need to bring.* **(~150–200 words.)**

## Mini-task
Write the **same three sentences** twice: once for a **friend**, once for the **headteacher**. Highlight every difference in word choice. That contrast *is* W4.

## Marking guide
`Purpose/form /5` — conventions of the form present; `Register /5` — consistent and matched to audience; `Coverage /5` — every bullet handled and developed.
