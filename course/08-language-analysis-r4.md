# 08 — Language Analysis: How Writers Create Effects (R4)

*Week 8. **First Language (0500) route mainly.** R4 is the objective students most often drop — and it carries part of two separate Paper 1 questions. If he is on the ESL route, this module still sharpens his reading but is not directly examined.*

---

## Learning goals
- Spot the writer's **techniques** (word choice, imagery, sentence structure, tone).
- Write about **effect on the reader**, not just *name* the device.
- Use the **quote → effect** pattern fluently.

---

## The core mistake

**Weak (naming):** *The writer uses a metaphor here.*
**Strong (effect):** *The metaphor "a sheet of beaten metal" makes the harbour sound hard and motionless, so the view feels frozen and slightly cold, and we sense the boy finds it beautiful but distant.*

Naming the device earns **nothing**. Explaining what it makes the reader *feel/see/think* earns the mark.

---

## The method: Q-E-E

**Quote** the exact words → **Explain** the technique/meaning → **Effect** on the reader (or on the mood).

**Example:**
> *"the old tram rattles up the hill as if it has all the time in the world"*
> **Q:** "as if it has all the time in the world"
> **E:** personifies the tram, giving it a leisurely human attitude, and the verb "rattles" suggests age and weariness.
> **Effect:** it makes the climb feel unhurried and timeless, so the reader slows down too — and we understand the boy's calm, familiar affection for a daily journey.

---

## What to look for (checklist)

| Technique | Ask |
|---|---|
| **Word choice** | Why *this* word? What connotation does it carry? |
| **Imagery** (simile/metaphor/personification) | What picture? What mood? |
| **Sentence length** | Short = tension/impact. Long = calm/flow. Why here? |
| **Punctuation** | Dashes, ellipses, exclamation — what feel? |
| **Sound** | Alliteration, sibilance, repetition — what rhythm? |
| **Tone/register** | Formal? Chatty? Ironic? |
| **Structure** | What is placed first/last? What builds up? |
