# 07 — Composition: Descriptive & Narrative

*Week 7. W1 (experience/expression) + W3 (vocabulary/sentences) + W2 (structure). On Paper 2 Section B he chooses from **four titles: two descriptive, two narrative.** Know which he's stronger at — and why.*

---

## Learning goals
- Plan a descriptive piece using the **senses + one controlling impression**.
- Plan a narrative using a **clear arc** (situation → complication → turning point → resolution).
- Control **pace** with sentence length and paragraph breaks.
- Avoid the **three killers**: no plan, clichéd endings, flat characters.

---

## Part A — Descriptive writing

**The controlling idea.** Decide *one* impression the place/scene should leave: *peaceful, threatening, chaotic, nostalgic*. Every detail chosen should serve it. Random detail = no marks.

**The senses frame** — before writing, list:
- **What he sees** (3 details, one unusual)
- **Hears** (2)
- **Smells or tastes** (1)
- **Feels on the skin** (1)
Then **zoom in** on one tiny thing and **zoom out** to the whole scene.

**Move the reader's eye** like a camera: wide shot → medium → close-up. Don't teleport.

**Model opening (HK wet market):**
> *Under the strip lights the market breathes. A hundred voices fold into one warm noise — the slap of a cleaver, the hiss of steam, a vendor's laugh bouncing off the wet floor. Somewhere at the back, where the light goes grey, a fish glints once, and is still.*

Notice: sensory, an unusual verb ("breathes"), one controlling impression (life, warmth), and no clichés.

**Practice:** plan and write **one paragraph (80 words)** describing a **typhoon** approaching, using the senses frame and a single controlling impression (**dread**).

---

## Part B — Narrative writing

**The arc:**
1. **Situation** — who, where, when. In *early*.
2. **Complication** — the problem arrives. Raise a question.
3. **Turning point** — the moment things change.
4. **Resolution** — show the *change* in the character, not just an event.

**Show character through action, not description.** *He was brave* = telling. *He stepped between his sister and the dog* = showing.

**Pace control:**
- **Fast:** short sentences, action verbs, dialogue.
- **Slow:** long sentences, description, reflection.
Swap deliberately to make the reader's heart rate move with the story.

**Endings:** avoid *"…and then I woke up"* and *"…it was all a dream."* Instead, end on a small, earned change: a decision, an object noticed, a line of dialogue.

**Model (growth ending):**
> *He didn't go back to the arcade that week, or the next. When his grandmother asked why, he only shrugged — but he left the trophy on her shelf, where she could see it, and where, every morning, she did.*

**Practice:** plan and write the **turning point paragraph (80 words)** of a story titled *"The Shortcut"* — using one short sentence to punch the moment.

---

## The 3-minute plan (both types)

**Descriptive:** *Controlling impression → 6 sensory details → camera moves (wide→close).*
**Narrative:** *Situation → Complication → Turning point → Resolution + the word/line you must end on.*

## Week 7 mini-tasks
1. ★ **Timed composition:** 45 minutes, 350–450 words, choose one of: (a) *Describe a place where you feel completely safe*; (b) *Write a story that begins "The lights went out…"* Mark with the band descriptors.
2. Rewrite your **weakest paragraph** from that composition — then compare. The rewrite is where the marks live.

## Marking guide
`Content/ideas /5` — vivid, relevant, developed; `Structure /5` — clear arc or clear camera movement; `Vocab/sentences /5` — varied and precise; `Accuracy /5` — few errors.
