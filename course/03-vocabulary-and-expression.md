# 03 — Vocabulary & Precise Expression

*Week 3. The difference between a middle-band and a top-band script is rarely grammar — it is **word choice**. "Very nice" costs marks; "sizzling" earns them.*

---

## Learning goals
- Replace **weak, vague words** ("nice", "big", "very + adjective") with precise ones.
- Use **figurative language** (simile, metaphor, personification) *sparingly and accurately*.
- **Show, don't tell** — imply emotion through detail.

---

## Lesson 1 — Kill the weak words

| Weak | Stronger options |
|---|---|
| nice (food) | delicious, fragrant, savoury, moreish |
| big | enormous, towering, vast, colossal |
| very tired | exhausted, shattered, drained |
| very hot | scorching, sweltering, blistering |
| walked slowly | shuffled, trudged, ambled, drifted |
| said | muttered, snapped, whispered, insisted |

**Rule:** if you've written **"very + adjective"**, find the one word that already means it. *Very hungry → famished. Very cold → freezing.*

**Practice 1** — replace the bold word with a precise one (no repeats):
1. The queue for the Star Ferry was **very long**.
2. He was **very scared** during the typhoon.
3. The roast goose smelled **nice**.
4. She **walked** proudly onto the stage.
5. The exam was **very hard**.

*Sample answers in `10-answer-key.md`.*

---

## Lesson 2 — Figurative language

- **Simile** — *like / as*: *Her laugh was like a door slamming.*
- **Metaphor** — says it *is*: *The city was a furnace.*
- **Personification** — gives human qualities to things: *The wind clawed at the windows.*
- **Pathetic fallacy** — weather mirroring mood: dark clouds before bad news.

**Warning:** one strong image beats five weak ones. Clichés (*as busy as a bee*, *raining cats and dogs*) score **nothing** — examiners have read them a thousand times.

**Practice 2** — turn each dull sentence into one with a single strong image.
1. It was raining hard.
2. He was nervous before the interview.
3. The classroom was noisy.
4. It was a happy day.
*Models in `10-answer-key.md`.*

---

## Lesson 3 — Show, don't tell

**Telling:** *He was embarrassed.*
**Showing:** *A red flush crept up his neck; he studied his shoes as if they held the answers.*

Showing works by giving the reader **evidence** (a gesture, a detail, a sensation) and letting them name the emotion.

**Practice 3** — convert:
1. She was excited. → (a gesture + a detail)
2. The old man was lonely. → (an object + an action)
3. I was angry. → (a physical sensation + a short sentence)

---

## Lesson 4 — Building a personal word bank

For each topic he writes about, collect an **8-word upgrade list**. Over 8 weeks, that is ~60 precise words he owns.

**Starter bank — Hong Kong scenes**
*weather:* humid, muggy, oppressive, drizzle, squall
*crowds:* jostling, bottleneck, throng, shoulder-to-shoulder
*food:* fragrant, glistening, crisp, chewy, steaming
*city:* neon, maze, fogbound, razor-thin, vertiginous (spire of towers)
*emotion:* dread, elation, resentment, relief, awe

**Practice 4** — write a **60-word paragraph** describing a Hong Kong street using **at least five** words from the bank above. Underline them.

## Mini-task (this week)
Take a piece of your Week 1 writing. Re-write it with **no sentence containing "very"** and **at least three precise verbs**. Compare the two versions side by side.

## Marking guide
`Word choice /5` — no vague fillers; `Imagery /5` — images fresh and accurate, not clichés.
