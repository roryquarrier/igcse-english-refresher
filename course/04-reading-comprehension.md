# 04 — Reading Comprehension

*Week 4. Covers R1 (explicit), R2 (implicit/attitudes), R5 (select for a purpose). R4 (effects) gets its own module — 08.*

---

## Learning goals
- Identify the **command word** and the **number of marks** before writing.
- Answer **explicit** questions exactly (R1) using the text's own detail.
- Read **implicit** meaning — attitude, tone, implication (R2).
- Support **every** point with a **quotation or reference**.

---

## Lesson 1 — De-code the question first

Underline the command word. It tells you the answer's shape:

| Command | What they want | Shape of answer |
|---|---|---|
| *Give / State* | one fact | a phrase |
| *Explain* | a reason, in your own words | because + point |
| *Describe* | detail from the text | 2–3 references |
| *How does the writer…* | analysis of effect (R4) | quote → effect |
| *Summarise* | selected points (R5) | bullet-proof prose, word-limited |
| *What impression…* | attitude/vibe (R2) | adjective + evidence |

**Marks = points.** A 3-mark question wants **three** separate ideas, each with support. Never write one good sentence and stop.

---

## Lesson 2 — R1: explicit answers

Copy the *precise* detail — don't approximate. If the text says "about twelve", "around ten" loses the mark.

**Practice 1** — from Text A (Week 1 diagnostic):
Q: Where is the boy? (2)
Model: *The boy is on an old tram that is climbing up the hill (1); he is inside it, pressing his forehead against the warm glass (1).*

---

## Lesson 3 — R2: implicit meaning and attitude

Watch the **signals**: *although, however, despite, unfortunately, but, even so*. These flip or colour the meaning. An attitude question usually needs an **adjective + evidence**.

**Practice 2** — from Text A: *"commuters don't look up."* What impression does this give? (3)
Model: *It shows the journey is ordinary for locals (1); they are so used to the route that they ignore it (1); only the tourists, "raising their phones", find it worth attention (1).*

---

## Lesson 4 — R5/main idea: pull it together

For "what is the text mainly about" style questions, name the **subject** and the writer's **angle on it**, not just the subject.

## Practice text — "The Cheung Chau Bun Scramble"

> Every year, on an island an hour's ferry ride from the city, a tower of buns rises in a village square. At midnight a whistle blows and dozens of climbers throw themselves at it, hauling themselves up the bamboo scaffolding while the crowd roars. They are not paid. They are not famous. For most of the year they are students, or chefs, or delivery riders. But for those three minutes, on one night in May, they are heroes of an island that knows every one of their names.
>
> The tradition nearly vanished. In 1978 the tower collapsed in a heap of bamboo and screaming men; forty people were hurt and the race was banned for years. When it returned, it came back small, careful, and wrapped in safety nets — and somehow, the village decided, even better. Not because danger is gone, but because the risk is now shared and understood rather than hidden.
>
> The buns themselves are not eaten. They are blessed, hauled down, and given away, which is, when you think about it, the whole point.

**Questions (20 marks total):**
1. What happens at midnight on the island? (2)
2. Using the passage, describe who the climbers are. (3)
3. What impression does the writer give of the climbers' everyday lives? (3)
4. Explain why the race was stopped in 1978. (3)
5. The writer says the race came back "even better". Explain what he means. (4)
6. Find one detail that suggests the island community is close-knit, and explain how it shows this. (5)
