# IGCSE English Refresher — 8-Week Bridge Course

> **Live site:** https://roryquarrier.github.io/igcse-english-refresher/ — the whole course as a browsable,
> mobile-friendly web app. The source markdown lives in `course/`; the site in `docs/` is generated from it by `docs/build.py`.


**For:** a 12-year-old boy in Hong Kong (Year 8 / age 12)
**Builds towards:** Cambridge IGCSE **First Language English (0500)** *or* **English as a Second Language (0510/0511)** — the two paths fork, see below.
**Time to exam:** typically 3 years. This is a **refresher / foundation-builder**, not last-minute cramming.
**Format:** 8 weeks × 4 sessions/week × ~40 min, plus one timed mock at the end.
**Total written output:** ~10,000 words across short tasks and 3 extended pieces.

---

## 1. Which IGCSE is he actually heading for?

The two English IGCSEs are **separate qualifications — a school enters a candidate for one, not both.** Get this right first, or half the practice is aimed at the wrong target.

| | **First Language English (0500/0990)** | **English as a Second Language (0510/0511/0991/0993)** |
|---|---|---|
| For | Students who use English as a *first* language | Students using English alongside a strong first language (Cantonese/Mandarin) |
| Papers | **Paper 1 Reading** (2h, 80 marks, 50%)<br>**Paper 2 Directed Writing & Composition** (2h, 80 marks, 50%) | **Paper 1 Reading & Writing**<br>**Paper 2 Listening**<br>**Paper 3 Speaking** (0511 = count-in) |
| Skills | Reading + writing only; expects native-level style and analysis | Reading, writing, **listening, speaking** |
| Speaking | Optional Component 4, separately endorsed (not in the grade) | Examined (0511) or optional (0510) |
| Typical HK schools | International schools, ESF, most through-train IB schools | Local / DSS schools, many EMI schools |

**Which one is he doing?** Ask his school. If the answer is unclear, run the **Week 1 diagnostic** — a lot of strong HK students sit between the two, and the diagnostic tells you which band of work to set.

**This course covers the skills shared by both, then forks in Weeks 5–7** (the directed-writing text types differ: 0500 does letter/article/speech/journal/report from a *reading* stimulus; ESL does email/report/article/review/letter from *bullet cues*).

## 2. The mark schemes to aim at (0500)

If he is on the **First Language** route, every question he answers is buying one or more of ten Assessment Objectives. Learn them by number — they tell him exactly what a question wants.

**Reading**
- **R1** — understanding of **explicit** meanings (what the text says)
- **R2** — understanding of **implicit** meanings and attitudes (what it means)
- **R3** — analyse, evaluate and develop facts, ideas and opinions, using support from the text
- **R4** — how writers **achieve effects** and influence readers (word choice, imagery, sentence structure) ← most often dropped by students
- **R5** — select and use information for a specific purpose (summary)

**Writing**
- **W1** — articulate experience; express what is thought, felt and imagined
- **W2** — organise and structure for deliberate effect (paragraphing, linking)
- **W3** — range of vocabulary and sentence structures
- **W4** — language appropriate to **purpose** and **audience**
- **W5** — accurate **spelling, punctuation, grammar** ← easiest to lose, five minutes at the end protects it

**Paper 1 (Reading), 2h, 80 marks:** four questions of 20 marks on three texts (A, B, C) — comprehension, summary, a language/effects task, and an extended response. Two of the four also carry writing marks.

**Paper 2 (Directed Writing & Composition), 2h, 80 marks:** Section A (40) — directed writing from a short text stimulus; Section B (40) — composition chosen from **four titles (two descriptive, two narrative)**.

## 3. The 8-week plan

Four sessions a week. Each session = warm-up (5 min) + teaching/practice (30 min) + mini-task (5 min). Marker "★" = do a timed version.

| Week | Module | Focus | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 01 | **Diagnostic test** + set goals | Scored diagnostic, target band agreed |
| **2** | 02 | **Grammar & punctuation foundations** | 2× error-correction sets |
| **3** | 03 | **Vocabulary & precise expression** | "Replace the weak word" set + 20-word upgrade list |
| **4** | 04 | **Reading comprehension** (R1/R2/R5) | One 20-mark comprehension |
| **5** | 05 | **Summary skills** (R5) | Two summaries in the exact pattern |
| **6** | 06 | **Directed writing** (W2/W4) | One directed piece (letter/article) |
| **7** | 07 | **Composition** — descriptive + narrative (W1/W3) | ★ One timed composition |
| **8** | 08 + 09 | **Language analysis (R4)** + **timed mock paper** | ★ Full mock under exam time |

**Weekly rhythm**
- **Session 1** — teach the week's skill; work the *worked examples*.
- **Session 2** — practice exercises; mark together with the answer key.
- **Session 3** — apply to the week's *mini-task*.
- **Session 4** — review errors; re-do the two hardest questions from memory.

**Marking shorthand used throughout this course** (keep a running score sheet in `progress.md`):
`/5` for each skill area, tick-list style, so progress is visible week to week.

## 5. Ground rules that actually raise the grade

1. **Every written answer is drafted, then edited.** Never hand in a first draft. Editing is where W5 marks live.
2. **Read the question twice; underline the command word** (*describe, explain, summarise, how does the writer…*). Most lost marks are misread questions, not weak English.
3. **Quote or point to the text** in every reading answer. "It says the bridge is old" beats "It is old".
4. **Plan for 3 minutes before any extended writing.** Five-line plan = two marks of structure.
5. **Time-box by marks:** roughly 1.5 minutes per mark in reading, and stick to it.
